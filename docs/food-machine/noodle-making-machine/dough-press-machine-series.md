## II. Production Process & Standard Operating Procedures (SOP)

Below is the standard operating procedure for setup, thickness calibration, dough sheeting, and routine maintenance:

**1. Pre-Operation Inspection & Setup**

* Position the machine on a flat, load-bearing floor and adjust the leveling feet so the unit is completely stable.
* Verify that the power supply (220V or 380V) is properly grounded and that the safety guard and emergency stop switch function correctly.
* Inspect the roller surface and receiving trays to ensure they are clean and free of debris.

**2. Thickness Clearance Calibration (5–20 mm)**

* Rotate the adjustment handwheel on the side of the machine to set the roller gap according to the graduated indicator.
* **Operating Tip**: When starting with a large, unworked dough block, set the clearance to maximum (approx. 15–20 mm). As the dough sheet becomes smoother and more compact, gradually narrow the gap to your target thickness (5–10 mm).

**3. Continuous Sheeting & Folding**

* Press the start button to begin low-speed roller rotation.
* Place the prepared dough on the feeding tray and guide the leading edge into the rollers. The machine will draw the dough in and sheet it.
* Receive the dough sheet on the output tray, dust lightly with dry flour, fold it in half, and re-feed it. Repeat 3–6 times until the sheet is smooth, glossy, and elastic.

**4. Shutdown Maintenance & Daily Care**

* After completing operations, press the stop button and disconnect the main power supply.
* Use a dry brush or scraper to clean off residual flour and dough bits from the rollers and trays, then wipe down the exterior with a damp cloth.
* **Safety Warning**: **Never spray water directly into the motor housing or electrical control enclosure!** Periodically (monthly) inspect the drive chain and apply a moderate amount of food-grade grease.

## IV. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

* **Q: How do I choose between the PRE-350 and PRE-500 models?**
  * **A**: Both models share the same 3.0 kW power and 114 mm roller diameter. The primary differences are the **sheeting width** and **voltage**: Choose the **PRE-350 (330 mm width, 220V)** if you have limited kitchen space or only 220V single-phase power. Choose the **PRE-500 (460 mm width, 380V)** if you operate a high-volume food plant or central kitchen with 380V 3-phase power, as its wider roller accommodates larger dough batches for higher output.
* **Q: Is the 5–20 mm clearance suitable for making thin dumpling skins or fine noodles directly?**
  * **A**: The PRE Series is designed as a **heavy-duty primary dough sheeter and kneading machine for large dough blocks and thicker dough sheets (5–20 mm)**, ideal for buns, steamed bread, flatbreads, and laminated pastries. For ultra-thin 1–2 mm dumpling skins or fine noodles, use the PRE machine for primary kneading and sheeting, then feed the sheet into a downstream precision machine (such as the BD Series or SK-080 wrapper maker) for final forming and cutting.
* **Q: What are the main advantages of a single gear chain drive over a belt drive?**
  * **A**: Heavy dough pressing generates substantial mechanical resistance. Belt drives tend to slip, overheat, and stretch or break under these conditions. The PRE Series uses an industrial single gear chain drive with direct sprocket engagement, delivering 100% positive power transfer with zero slippage, ensuring durable operation over years of high-load commercial use.
