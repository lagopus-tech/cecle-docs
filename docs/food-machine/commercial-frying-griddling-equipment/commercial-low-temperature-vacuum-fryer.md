
# Commercial Low-Temperature Vacuum Fryer

The CECLE Commercial Industrial Low-Temperature Vacuum Fryer Series (covering the standard commercial **ZK-500** and medium-to-large industrial line models **ZK-350 / ZK-700 / ZK-1200**) is high-end vacuum low-temperature dehydration processing equipment engineered for healthy fruit and vegetable chip processing plants, snack food manufacturers, agricultural cooperative deep-processing bases, and central kitchens. It completely overcomes the traditional atmospheric high-temperature frying (160°C–220°C) bottlenecks that destroy natural vitamins, cause scorching, darkening, and enzymatic browning, produce excessive oil absorption (30%–40%), and generate carcinogenic acrylamide. Featuring an innovative architecture combining **-0.090 to -0.098 MPa high-vacuum negative pressure + 80°C–115°C prime low-temperature frying + in-situ dynamic high-speed centrifugal de-oiling under vacuum**, moisture inside raw ingredients rapidly vaporizes and expands at only 40°C–60°C, generating an airy, porous, honeycomb-crisp cellular structure. Ingredients undergo dynamic centrifugal oil removal inside the sealed vacuum chamber before discharging, **dropping the finished product oil content down to 5%–15% while locking in vibrant natural pigmentation, original flavor profiles, and bioactive nutrients (such as Vitamin C and beta-carotene)**. Built entirely from food-grade thickened SUS304 stainless steel with automated PLC touch-screen controls, it is the premier industrial solution for producing "clean-label, low-oil, ultra-crisp, additive-free" premium fruit and vegetable crisps (banana, apple, okra, winter jujube, shiitake mushroom, and carrot chips).

### 2. Core Functions & Operating Principles

The system operates on the scientific principles of **Vacuum Vaporization** and **Micro-Porous Texture Puffing**:

* **Depression of Water's Boiling Point**: At atmospheric pressure, water boils at 100°C (212°F). Under a high vacuum of -0.098 MPa, water's boiling point plummets to 40°C–50°C (104°F–122°F).
* **Gentle Heat Transfer & Instantaneous Dehydration**: Using food-grade vegetable oil heated to 85°C–105°C (185°F–221°F) as a high-efficiency thermal conductor, internal cellular moisture boils and flashes into vapor at gentle temperatures. The escaping water vapor gently expands natural plant fibers, forming a micro-porous matrix that delivers a delightfully light, crispy bite.
* **Oxygen-Deficient Environment Prevents Browning**: The near-vacuum chamber starves polyphenol oxidase (PPO) of oxygen. As a result, chlorophyll, carotenoids, and anthocyanins remain unoxidized and vibrant, eliminating the need for sulfur dioxide or artificial color preservatives.

## 2. Standard Operating Procedures (SOP) & Production Workflow

To ensure maximum puffing crispness, maintain high operating vacuum, and protect thermal oil quality, strictly observe the following SOP:

### 1. Raw Ingredient Pre-Processing (Critical Steps)

* **Washing & Uniform Slicing**: Thoroughly wash and slice ingredients uniformly (e.g., banana slices at 4–6 mm; sweet potato batons at 8–10 mm; okra with caps trimmed while keeping the pod intact).
* **Blanching & Sugar Infusion/De-Astringency**: Depending on the produce, perform short-duration hot water blanching at 85°C–95°C for 1–2 minutes to deactivate enzymes. Tart fruits may undergo vacuum sugar infusion to balance acidity.
* **Ultra-Low Temperature Pre-Freezing (Critical Success Factor)**:
  * **Drain produce thoroughly and freeze in a blast freezer at -18°C to -25°C (-0.4°F to -13°F) for 4–8 hours until rock-solid!**
  * *Mechanism*: Water within the cell matrix crystallizes into microscopic ice crystals. When exposed to vacuum thermal oil, these crystals sublimate instantly into vapor, propping open a porous cellular framework that prevents shrinkage, curl, or collapse.

### 2. Pre-Start Inspection & Piping Setup

1. **Power & Pneumatic Supply**: Connect to 380V 3-phase industrial power via dedicated circuit breakers. Ensure the pneumatic supply delivers 0.6–0.8 MPa of clean, dry air for solenoid valves.
2. **Water-Ring Vacuum Pump Cooling Flow**: Verify water levels and circulation flow from the external cooling tower to the vacuum pump reservoir, ensuring cooling water temperature remains below 30°C (cooler water produces higher ultimate vacuum).
3. **Thermal Oil Filling**: Fill the tank with thermally stable vegetable oil (refined palm oil, coconut oil, or high-oleic soybean oil) up to the marked operating level.

### 3. Standardized Production Workflow (e.g., Banana / Sweet Potato Chips)

1. **Basket Loading & Chamber Locking**: Distribute frozen ingredients evenly across tiered stainless steel mesh trays (avoid overfilling or compacting). Hoist the basket frame into the chamber and lock the vessel door dogs securely.
2. **One-Touch Recipe Activation (PLC Selection)**:
   * Select the product profile on the touch screen. Set target vacuum (typically ≤ -0.095 MPa), oil temperature (e.g., 85°C–90°C for bananas; 95°C–105°C for root vegetables), frying duration (30–50 minutes), and centrifugal spin parameters (300–500 rpm for 3–5 minutes).
3. **Vacuum Evacuation & Oil Immersion**:
   * The vacuum pump engages. Once internal pressure reaches set threshold levels, the pneumatic elevator submerges the product basket into the thermal oil. Water evaporates rapidly, and condensates are continuously purged by the condenser.
4. **In-Situ Vacuum Centrifugal De-Oiling**:
   * Upon completion of frying, the elevator lifts the basket out of the oil while *maintaining full vacuum*. The variable-frequency drive initiates high-speed forward/reverse centrifugal spinning, slinging excess oil off the product.
5. **Atmospheric Venting & Discharge**:
   * The vent solenoid opens, restoring the chamber to atmospheric pressure. Open the vessel door, hoist out the basket, and transfer hot crisps immediately to a fluidized cooling screen before packaging in nitrogen-flushed barrier bags.

### 4. Shutdown, Oil Filtration & Routine Maintenance

* **Vacuum Oil Precision Filtration**: Run the circulating fine-mesh oil filter after each production run to capture microscopic suspended sugars and carbon fines, preventing increases in acid value.
* **Condensate Draining**: Open the bottom drain valve on the vapor-liquid separator after every shift to completely empty accumulated condensate, preventing water backflow into the vacuum pump.
* **Vacuum Seals & Pump Care**: Check water-ring pump packing and oil levels periodically. Inspect high-tear silicone door gaskets before closing, lubricating lightly with food-grade silicone grease to extend seal life.

## 4. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

* **Q: What is the fundamental difference between low-temperature vacuum frying and conventional atmospheric deep frying?**
  * **A**: They differ substantially across three dimensions:
    1. **Operating Temperature**: Conventional frying operates at 160°C–220°C (320°F–428°F), degrading vitamins, scorching sugars, and generating acrylamide carcinogens. Low-temperature vacuum frying lowers water's boiling point under high negative pressure, holding oil temperatures at a gentle 85°C–105°C (185°F–221°F) to gently dehydrate rather than scorch.
    2. **Oil Content**: Atmospherically fried goods absorb 35%–45% oil upon exiting the fryer. Vacuum frying utilizes in-situ vacuum centrifugal spinning, bringing final oil content down to 8%–15% for a crisp, non-greasy bite.
    3. **Color & Nutrient Retention**: The near-absence of oxygen prevents thermal oxidation and enzymatic browning, retaining nearly 100% of the produce's natural color, aromatic volatiles, and vitamins without artificial preservatives.
* **Q: Why must centrifugal de-oiling take place *under vacuum* before breaking the chamber pressure?**
  * **A**: This is the core engineering hurdle of the vacuum-frying process. When frying concludes, the microscopic cellular pores of the produce are filled with low-pressure water vapor. If the chamber were vented to atmospheric pressure before spinning, external ambient air would act like a piston, forcing surface oil deep into the porous matrix where it cannot be removed. Conducting centrifugal de-oiling *within the vacuum envelope* removes surface oil while pores remain open, ensuring low oil retention once vented.
* **Q: Why is blast-freezing ingredients to -18°C strongly recommended prior to vacuum frying?**
  * **A**: Fresh, unfrozen produce contains free-moving water that boils out erratically under vacuum, causing shrinkage, curling, case-hardening, and a tough, leathery texture. Blast-freezing locks cellular moisture into uniform microscopic ice crystals. In warm vacuum oil, these ice crystals sublimate rapidly into vapor, propping open an airy, structural cellular framework that yields flat, unbroken chips with a light, melt-in-the-mouth crunch.
