### 1.2 Core Functions & Working Principle

The machine utilizes a synchronized mechanical process of **"flow-line posture restraint + dynamic high-speed rotary slitting"**:

* **Infeed & Self-Centering Alignment**: The operator places fresh or chilled whole fish (either descaled or scaled) head-first (or tail-first depending on recipe requirements) into the V-guide trough with the spine or belly facing correctly. Cutting depth stops precisely at the spinal centerline, leaving the gallbladder 100% intact before the fish smoothly slides out the discharge chute into collection crates.

## 2. Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)

To maintain worker safety, extend blade and bearing longevity, and preserve strict food sanitation, strictly adhere to the following protocols:

### 2.1 Pre-Start Inspection & Utility Connection

1. **Placement & Leveling**: Position the machine on a level, slip-resistant processing floor with ample drainage. Rotate the 4 heavy-duty stainless steel leveling feet until the chassis is completely stable without rocking.
2. **Electrical Connection (⚠️ Crucial Safety Point)**:
   * Rated motor power is 2.2 kW (2.95 HP), configured for 220V single-phase (or optional 380V 3-phase / 110V export voltages).
   * **Must be wired to a dedicated circuit breaker with leakage protection, ensuring the grounding wire is securely fastened to ground**; never let power cords sit submerged in standing floor water.
3. **Discharge Chute & Collection Setup**: Position standard perforated food-grade plastic bins beneath the discharge chute, and arrange the downstream gutting/washing line immediately adjacent.

### 2.2 Blade Depth Adjustment & Daily Operation

1. **Pre-Setting Blade Depth by Weight**: Before running, calibrate the top adjustment handle according to the primary fish weight processed that day (e.g., 0.5 kg tilapia vs. 1.5 kg grass carp):
   * **Smaller Fish (0.1–0.5 kg)**: Rotate clockwise to lower the blade carriage to prevent incomplete slitting;
   * **Larger Fish (1.0–2.5 kg)**: Rotate counter-clockwise to raise the carriage slightly, cutting through thick dorsal muscle while clearing abdominal viscera.
2. **Power-On Test & Idle Check**: Turn on the main power supply and press the green "START" button. Verify the conveyor chain advances smoothly in the correct direction and the rotary blade spins freely without vibration, scraping, or abnormal metallic noise. Run idle for 10 seconds before feeding fish.
3. **Standardized Continuous Infeed Technique**:
   * Wear non-slip, cut-resistant food processing gloves;
   * Grasp each whole fish with both hands, align the head forward with the spine or belly centered into the V-chain groove, and release your grip, letting the conveyor feed the fish into the chamber;
   * **Absolute Safety Red Line (⚠️)**: **Never reach your hands inside the safety shield opening while the machine is running! In the event of a jam, immediately hit the red EMERGENCY STOP palm button, disconnect the power source, and clear the blockage using a long hook tool!**

### 2.4 End-of-Day Sanitation & Blade Maintenance

* **Daily Washdown Procedure**:
  1. Once processing concludes, press the red STOP button and switch off the main facility breaker;
  2. Lift the quick-release tool-less inspection canopy on top;
  3. Use a low-to-medium-pressure water spray nozzle to flush accumulated fish scales, blood, and slime off the conveyor links, guide plates, and cutting zone;
  4. Spray all contact surfaces with neutral food-grade sanitizer, let soak for 3–5 minutes, rinse thoroughly with fresh water, and allow to air dry.
* **Alloy Blade Care (Weekly Requirement)**:
  * Regularly inspect the rotary blade's cutting edge. If handling hard-boned fish causes minor dulling, remove the blade and hone it with a fine diamond whetstone;
  * After cleaning and drying, wipe a thin coat of **food-grade white mineral oil (anti-rust oil)** across the blade surface and drive chains to prevent oxidation.

## 4. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

* **Q: What is the fundamental difference between the Open Back and Open Belly models? Can one machine switch between both?**
  * **A**: The primary differences lie in the **blade entry angle, internal guide curvature, and overhead contour molds**:
    * **Open Back Model**: Engineered specifically to split along the dorsal spine, allowing the fish to fold open completely flat for grilled fish restaurants, barbecue spots, and cured fish processors;
    * **Open Belly Model**: Engineered to slice precisely along the ventral midline, enabling rapid manual gutting, evisceration, and gill removal for fresh markets, supermarket counters, and fish filleting plants;
    * **Buying Advice**: The internal geometry is factory-calibrated and rigidly locked for each slitting direction to prevent blade drift and burst gallbladders. **We strongly recommend selecting either the dedicated Open Back or Open Belly model based on your menu before ordering**.
* **Q: Will the machine puncture the fish's gallbladder and make the meat bitter?**
  * **A**: Absolutely not! This is the standout engineering highlight of the FFK-18. With calibrated micrometer dials, the cutting edge penetrates strictly along the bone-muscle junction without piercing the internal peritoneal sac, while flexible guide plates gently push the viscera away from the blade path. When adjusted according to the operation manual, **gallbladder rupture rates remain under 0.1%**.
* **Q: Do fish need to be descaled before running through this machine? Can scaled fish be cut directly?**
  * **A**: Both scaled and descaled fish can be cut directly! The high-torque industrial motor and cryo-quenched alloy disc deliver immense piercing force, effortlessly cleaving through thick, tough scales on fish like black carp and snakehead. However, for commercial restaurant presentation, descaling fish in a commercial rotary descaler first is recommended for cleaner processing downstream.
