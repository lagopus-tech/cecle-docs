## 2. Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)

To maintain electrical safety, extend bearing and motor service life, and ensure consistent beverage quality, strictly adhere to the following protocols:

### 2.1 Pre-Start Inspection & Electrical Installation

1. **Placement & Clearance**: Place the base on a level, dry, stable countertop. Maintain at least **5–10 cm (2–4 inches)** of ventilation clearance around the base and rear. Never block the bottom air cooling intake with bar towels or packaging.
2. **Electrical Safety Connection (⚠️ Crucial Safety Point)**:
   * The HS-200 draws up to 2200W, supporting **110V–120V / 220V–240V (50/60Hz)** electrical configurations;
   * Connect directly to an independent, grounded wall receptacle rated for the load. Never daisy-chain with ice makers, espresso machines, or water boilers on unrated multi-outlet power strips.
3. **Safety Interlock Micro-Switch Check**: The motor base features a built-in safety interlock switch. **The jar must be fully seated on the centering pad before the motor will engage**; removing the jar cuts power instantly to prevent accidents.

### 2.2 Variable Speed & Pulse Operation Protocols

1. **Ingredient Loading Order (Golden Operational Rule)**:
   * **Sequence**: Pour liquids first (water, milk, syrups) ➔ add soft ingredients (fresh fruit, yogurt) ➔ place hard solids, nuts, and ice cubes on top;
   * Never run dry without liquid. Do not exceed the **MAX 2000 ml** line for cold drinks (limit hot liquids to **1200 ml** to allow expansion).
2. **Stepped Speed Ramp Technique**:
   * Seal the silicone lid and lock the center transparent measuring cap;
   * Flip on the power switch, **start at low speed (Setting 1–3) for 2–3 seconds** to draw ingredients down into the blade vortex, then smoothly rotate the dial to high speed (Setting 7–10);
   * Never switch on directly at maximum speed, avoiding excessive torque shocks to drive couplings.
3. **Pulse Function Usage**:
   * For dense ice blocks or whole frozen fruit, push down the "Pulse" toggle for 1–2 seconds and release, repeating 3–4 times for initial breakdown before running continuously on high.

**Safety & Hot Liquid Warning (⚠️)**:
* When blending liquids above 60°C (140°F), **never exceed 1.2L capacity**. Vent the center lid insert slightly or hold a thick dry towel over the lid to relieve thermal steam pressure.
* To add ingredients mid-cycle, remove only the central clear filler plug. **Never insert fingers, spatulas, or metal spoons into the jar while the motor is spinning!**

### 2.4 End-of-Day Cleaning & Maintenance

* **10-Second High-Speed Self-Cleaning**:
  1. Immediately after dispensing, fill the jar 1/3 full with lukewarm water and add 1–2 drops of neutral dish soap;
  2. Secure the lid, seat the jar, tap "Pulse" 3 times, then run on high speed for **10–15 seconds**;
  3. The high-speed vortex scours the container walls and blade recesses. Pour out the water and rinse clean.
* **Blade Bearing Care Warnings**:
  * **Never submerge the entire jar base in a sink or soak it overnight!** The bottom houses precision alloy bearings and water seals; soaking leaches out internal grease, causing stiff rotation and seal failure.
  * Invert the jar on a drying rack to air-dry naturally after rinsing.

## 4. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

* **Q: Can the HS-200 blend ice continuously in high-volume environments without burning the motor?**
  * **A**: Yes. The HS-200 is built specifically for commercial beverage stations. It combines a heavy-duty copper motor with bottom cross-flow ventilation channels and a **dual thermal overload protection system**. It maintains stable performance through summer peak rushes; in the event of an obstruction-induced current surge, safety switches trip automatically to protect the windings, resetting once cooled.
* **Q: Why are ice cubes spinning above the blades without being drawn down?**
  * **A**: This is common blender **cavitation (air pocketing)**, typically caused by insufficient liquid or improper loading sequence. To resolve:
    1. Always add liquids first to establish a fluid vortex base;
    2. Insert the included **safety tamper stick** through the lid opening to press floating ice down into the cutting stream;
    3. Tap the "Pulse" switch 2–3 times before switching to continuous high speed.
* **Q: How can I tell when blade bearings need maintenance or replacement?**
  * **A**: After 6–12 months of heavy use, if the jar emits a high-pitched grinding sound when spun or shows small traces of lubricant underneath, the bearing seal has undergone natural wear. The entire blade assembly can be unscrewed and replaced with a factory kit without replacing the entire container.
