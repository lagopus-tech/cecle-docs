## 2. Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)

To maintain peak grinding efficiency and maximize the lifespan of internal components, adhere to the following procedures:

### 2.1 Food Preparation & Pre-Processing

* **Deboning & Inspection**: Thoroughly remove hard bones, cartilage, and heavy connective tissue before grinding. (Never feed hard bones or unthawed frozen meat into the unit, as this may chip the blade or overload the motor.)
* **Pre-Slicing**: Slice fresh meat into strips approximately 2.5–4 cm wide so they slide freely into the feed tube. If making sausage, ensure mince is pre-seasoned and casings are pre-soaked.

### 2.2 Assembly & Pre-Operation Check

1. Ensure the power cord is unplugged and the power switch is in the **OFF** position.
2. Insert the grinding barrel into the front housing socket and tighten the side locking screw securely.
3. Slide the screw auger into the barrel, ensuring the rear hexagonal drive pin engages fully with the motor transmission socket.
4. Mount the cross blade onto the drive shaft with the **cutting edges facing outward toward the hole plate**. Seat the grinding plate flush against the blade.
5. Screw the front retaining ring clockwise until hand-tight. (Do not overtighten with mechanical tools to avoid premature blade wear.)

### 2.3 Grinding Operation

* Connect the machine to a matching power supply (110V or 220V).
* Flip the switch to start the motor. Allow the machine to idle for a few seconds to verify smooth rotation and absence of abnormal noise.
* Feed pre-cut meat strips into the hopper. Always use the **supplied safety food pusher** to advance meat down the chute. **Never insert fingers or utensils into the feed throat.**
* Place a clean container beneath the discharge head to catch the extruded meat.

### 2.4 Sausage Stuffing Operation

* Remove the cross blade and grinding plate. Install the sausage stuffing spacer/flange and select the appropriate stuffing tube nozzle, then secure the front ring.
* Slide the soaked casing over the stuffing tube, pulling a small tail out and tying a knot.
* Switch on the machine and feed seasoned ground meat into the chute. Guide the casing by hand to control stuffing firmness, then twist or tie off into links.

### 2.5 Shutdown, Disassembly & Maintenance

* Turn off the machine and disconnect the power plug.
* Unscrew the front locking ring, then remove the plate, cross blade, screw auger, and grinding tube in sequence.
* Wash all removable food-contact components with warm water and a neutral food-safe detergent. Dry completely with a clean cloth or air dry.
* Apply a thin film of food-grade mineral or cooking oil to the cross blade and grinding plates to prevent surface oxidation, then store in a dry, ventilated area.

## 4. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

* **Q: Is the 150 kg/h capacity of the CMG-150 suitable for commercial or household use?**
  * **A**: The CMG-150 is a versatile crossover unit. With an output of up to 150 kg/h (approx. 330 lbs/h), it easily handles daily meat prep in restaurants, dumpling shops, butcher delis, and small farms. At the same time, its compact countertop footprint makes it ideal for home users who regularly process large batches of venison, seasonal sausages, or pet food.
* **Q: Can the CMG-150 handle meat with light cartilage or sinew? Will it jam?**
  * **A**: Powered by an 800W motor paired with a 170 / 220 r/min cutting speed, the CMG-150 delivers strong shear torque to process meat containing light sinew and soft connective tissue without stalling. However, large hard bones and rock-hard frozen blocks must not be fed into the machine to protect blade sharpness and gear longevity.
* **Q: How should the grinding components be cleaned and maintained against rust?**
  * **A**: The grinding head assembly (tube, screw auger, cross blade, and hole plate) can be disassembled by hand in seconds. Clean all parts using warm water and mild dish soap, then dry thoroughly with a towel. Applying a thin coating of cooking oil to the blade and hole plate surfaces after drying will protect them against oxidation over long storage periods.
