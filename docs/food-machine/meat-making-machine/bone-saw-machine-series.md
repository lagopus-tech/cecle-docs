## II. Production Workflow & Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)

To maintain maximum operator safety and prolong the working life of the band saw blade, adhere strictly to the following standard operating procedures:

### 1. Ingredient Preparation & Pre-Treatment

* **Suitable Materials**: Deep-frozen meats (optimally tempered between -18°C and -2°C), large beef femur bones, lamb chops, pork ribs, T-bone steaks, tomahawk cuts, frozen whole poultry, frozen ribbonfish/salmon portions, etc.
* **Pre-Cut Sizing**: Ensure the height and width of raw cuts fit comfortably within the working envelope of the machine (ingredient clearance height should not exceed 20 cm) to avoid jamming against the upper blade guide.

### 2. Pre-Start Inspection & Blade Tension Adjustment

1. Verify the unit is placed on a level, weight-bearing surface and connected to a properly grounded 110V or 220V power supply.
2. Open the housing door and verify blade tooth orientation (**teeth must point downward toward the table**). Check that the blade rests securely in the guides of the upper and lower wheels and between the wear-resistant guide blocks.
3. Turn the top tension knob to calibrate blade tightness:
   * **Turn Clockwise**: Tightens/stretches the saw blade; lightly press the flat side of the blade with your finger—it should feel taut with slight resilience.
   * **Turn Counter-Clockwise**: Relieves tension for blade removal or replacement.
4. Close and securely latch the housing door. Run the machine empty for 10–15 seconds to ensure smooth, vibration-free tracking and proper motor rotation.

### 3. Standard Bone Sawing & Slicing Operation

1. Loosen the locking knob on the thickness guide plate, slide it to the desired scale reading (e.g., 20 mm for thick-cut steaks), and firmly re-tighten.
2. Press the green waterproof power switch to start the motor and allow the machine to reach full operational speed.
3. Position the frozen meat block or bone flat against the thickness guide plate.
4. **Always use the included safety push plate** pressed against the rear of the meat. Hold the safety handle with both hands and push smoothly toward the blade at a steady, controlled pace to complete the cut.
5. Once cut, move the finished portion into a collection tray, retract the push plate, and repeat for the next cut.

### 4. Tips for Replacing the Saw Blade

1. Always **disconnect the main power plug** before servicing or replacing components.
2. Unscrew the door latch knobs and open the machine housing.
3. **Turn the top adjustment handwheel counter-clockwise** to lower the upper wheel until the blade is fully slackened.
4. Carefully unhook and remove the worn blade. Thread the new blade into the upper and lower wheel tracks, confirming that the **blade teeth point downward toward the cutting table**.
5. **Turn the top handwheel clockwise** to tension the blade. Manually rotate the drive wheel to verify that the blade tracks centered on the wheels. Once proper tension is achieved, close the safety door and tighten the latch knobs.

### 5. Shutdown, Cleaning & Preventive Maintenance

* Disconnect power immediately after operations cease.
* Open the cabinet door and scrape out collected bone dust and meat trim from the base collection area.
* Clean the stainless steel table surface, guide plate, and safety pusher with warm water and mild, food-safe detergent, then dry thoroughly.
* After drying, wipe a light coat of food-grade mineral or vegetable oil over the blade, guide blocks, and bare metal contact surfaces to prevent moisture-induced surface corrosion.

## IV. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

* **Q: Can the BSM-1400 bone saw cut fresh, unfrozen soft meat?**
  * **A**: It is not recommended for completely unfrozen, room-temperature soft meats. High-speed band saws rely on fine-toothed blades running at high vertical velocity to shear rigid structures (such as bones, ribs, semi-frozen, or hard-frozen meat blocks). Cutting unfrozen soft meat can cause sinew and soft fibers to catch between the teeth and enter the wheel guide cavities, resulting in torn meat and potential jams. For fresh meat slicing and shredding, rotary slicers/dicers (such as the MG series) are recommended.
* **Q: How long does a saw blade last, and how do I adjust blade tension properly?**
  * **A**: Blade lifespan depends directly on daily production volume and bone density. Replace the blade when cutting feed resistance noticeably increases, cutting speed drops, or cut surfaces display tearing. Tension adjustment is straightforward: rotate the top handwheel clockwise to tighten and counter-clockwise to loosen. Proper tension is reached when pressing firmly against the side of the blade yields slight, firm flex. Avoid overtightening (which accelerates blade fatigue breakage) or leaving it too loose (which risks blade derailment).
* **Q: How does the machine ensure operator safety during continuous commercial use?**
  * **A**: The BSM-1400 incorporates multiple safety protections: ① A dedicated ergonomic pusher plate with an anti-slip grip ensures a physical barrier between the operator's hands and the saw blade at all times; ② Enclosed cabinet doors with dual locking screws prevent startup when doors are unlatched; ③ Industrial-grade waterproof power switches prevent electrical shock during wet-hand operation; ④ A stable 35 kg net weight and anti-skid foot pads suppress vibration and prevent machine drift.
