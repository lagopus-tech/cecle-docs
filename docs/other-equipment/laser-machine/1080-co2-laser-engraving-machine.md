## III. Operation Process

1. **Artwork Setup & Layer Assignment**: Import vector shapes or raster images into the software. Set engraving speeds (1-500mm/s), cutting speeds (1-50mm/s), and laser output power by layer color.
2. **Workpiece Loading & Focus Calibration**: Place the glass, crystal item, or sheet on the worktable (use an optional rotary attachment for cylindrical glassware). Set the focal distance using the focus gauge and verify boundaries with the red dot indicator.
3. **Auxiliary Systems Startup**: Turn on the water cooling unit, air compressor, and exhaust fan. Ensure normal cooling water temperature and consistent circulation before firing.
4. **Enclosed Processing & Part Unloading**: Close the safety lid and start the machine from the control panel. After processing, let the exhaust fan clear residual vapors before opening the lid to retrieve the finished piece.

### **Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)**
* **Q: Can the 1080 laser machine cut through glass and crystal plates?**  
  * A: No. CO2 lasers perform high-detail frosted surface engraving and etching on glass and crystal. They cannot slice or cut through glass plates; through-cutting requires high-pressure waterjets or diamond tools.
* **Q: Is a rotary attachment required for engraving cylindrical bottles or glasses?**  
  * A: Yes. For cylindrical or conical items like wine bottles and tumblers, an optional rotary roller attachment rotates the item synchronously for seamless 360-degree engraving.
* **Q: How should I choose between 60W, 80W, and 100W laser power?**  
  * A: For delicate photo engraving on crystal, fine typography, and thin crafts, 60W or 80W produces finer beam spots and minimal heat distortion. Choose 100W if you also need to cut 8mm to 15mm acrylic or thicker wooden boards.
