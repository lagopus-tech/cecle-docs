## III. Operation Process

1. **Design Import & Parameter Setup**: Prepare vector files and raster artwork in your design software. Assign cutting speeds (1-50 mm/s), engraving speeds (1-500 mm/s), and laser output percentages by color layer.
2. **Material Alignment & Focal Calibration**: Position the stock onto the honeycomb or aluminum knife-blade bed. Set the standard focal distance using the acrylic focus gauge, and check job perimeter framing using the red dot pointer.
3. **Auxiliary Systems Startup**: Turn on the industrial water chiller, air assist compressor, and exhaust fan. Verify steady coolant flow and confirm water temperature is within operating limits.
4. **Enclosed Execution & Safe Unloading**: Close the safety lid and run the job from the control panel. Once processing completes, let the exhaust fan evacuate residual smoke before lifting the lid to retrieve finished parts.

### **Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)**
* **Q: Can the 9060 CO2 laser cut bare metal sheets?**  
  * A: No. CO2 laser sources are engineered for non-metal materials like acrylic, plywood, MDF, paper, and leather. While it cannot melt or cut bare metal sheets, it can mark anodized aluminum or create permanent black marks on metals using specialized laser bonding sprays.
* **Q: Which laser power should I choose: 60W/80W or 100W/130W?**  
  * A: Select 60W or 80W if your work centers on fine photographic engraving and micro-lettering with small beam spots. Choose 100W or 130W if you frequently cut 8mm to 15mm+ thick acrylic or dense timber sheets.
* **Q: Can this machine be safely operated inside a home studio or small garage?**  
  * A: Yes. The fully enclosed cabinet prevents laser reflection, and the high-output exhaust blower routes fumes through exterior ducting or into an indoor filtration unit to maintain clean working air.
