## II. Production Workflow & Standard Operating Procedures (SOP)

### 1. Site Preparation & Pre-Installation Requirements (Key Success Factors)

* **Ventilation Clearance**: Maintain at least 4–6 inches (10–15 cm) of clearance around the compressor ventilation louvers and the rear panel. Never block the airflow vents with boxes or storage items.
* **Pre-Startup Resting Rule**: Upon arrival via freight, **DO NOT plug in or turn on the unit immediately!** Keep the unit upright on a level surface for **at least 24 hours**. This allows compressor oil displaced during transit to settle completely, preventing oil blockages that can degrade cooling performance or damage the compressor.

### 2. Pre-Startup Inspection & Workstation Setup

1. Position the ladder freezer along your planned service or prep line, away from open flames, cooking ranges, or direct sunlight. Engage the front caster brakes to lock it firmly in place.
2. Confirm your incoming voltage matches the rated specification (110V or 220V) and connect only to a dedicated, properly **grounded wall outlet**.
3. Slide open the tempered glass doors and inspect the 3-tier pans and guide rails to ensure tracks are free of debris and glide smoothly.

### 3. Standard Operating Guidelines

1. **Empty-Load Pre-Cooling**: Close the sliding doors, turn on power, and run empty for **1 to 2 hours**. Wait until the digital display confirms that the upper zone has reached 2°C–10°C and the lower zone has reached 0°C–-15°C before loading food.
2. **Organized Tier Loading**:
   * **Top & Middle Tiers**: Load with chilled items such as vegetables, mushrooms, pre-mixed sauces, cold appetizers, and high-turnover self-serve dishes.
   * **Bottom Tier & Deep Well**: Store frozen meats, raw seafood, meatballs, and frozen dumpling products.
   * **Important**: Do not stack products higher than the sliding door tracks, as this will compromise the air seal.
3. **No Hot Foods Allowed**: Cooked, blanched, or freshly fried foods must cool to room temperature in a preparation area before being placed inside the unit.

## IV. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

* **Q: Is frost buildup along the inner walls normal for a direct-cooling unit? How should it be defrosted?**
  * **A:** Yes, frost buildup is a completely normal physical occurrence in direct-cooling refrigeration. As doors are opened repeatedly during busy shifts, ambient humidity condenses and freezes against the cold interior walls. When the frost layer reaches approximately 5 mm (0.2 inches), simply turn off the power at the end of the day, allow the frost to melt naturally, drain through the bottom drainage outlet, and wipe dry with a soft cloth. Never scrape interior walls with sharp metal tools.
* **Q: Why choose an "Upper Refrigeration, Lower Freezing" stepped design?**
  * **A:** Naturally, cold air sinks while warm air rises. This layout works in harmony with physics: the upper tier provides gentle, uniform chilling that keeps vegetables crisp without freeze damage, while the dense cold air pools in the lower section to keep frozen proteins solid. This delivers both energy savings and integrated one-stop ingredient management.
* **Q: Will the tempered glass doors fog up in high-humidity weather and obscure the display?**
  * **A:** The doors use commercial-grade insulated tempered glass to minimize temperature transfer. Additionally, the door frames feature airflow-break thermal barrier channels that substantially reduce condensation. Under extreme humidity, a quick wipe with a clean microfiber cloth instantly restores full visibility.
