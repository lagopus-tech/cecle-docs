## II. Production Workflow & Standard Operating Procedures (SOP)

To ensure this 54-inch 2-door refrigerator runs stably for years and maintains optimal cooling performance, kitchen staff must strictly follow these operational rules:

### 1. Site Preparation & Pre-Treatment (Critical Success Factor)

* **Adequate Ventilation and Opening Clearance Must Be Ensured**:
  * Despite its compact footprint, its heavy-duty compressor generates significant heat—**never place the back of the unit tight against a wall!** Leave at least 4-6 inches (10-15 cm) of convection clearance at the back and top. Also ensure both doors can open fully past 90 degrees.
* **Static Placement Requirement Before Startup**:
  * After delivery, unboxing, and positioning, **DO NOT plug it in immediately!** Due to transit turbulence, lubricating oil inside the compressor may shift. The unit must remain upright and static for **at least 24 hours** to allow oil to fully return to the compressor base before powering on.

### 2. Pre-Start Inspection & Workstation Setup

1. Push the 54-inch commercial refrigerator to a flat, dry floor away from open flames/heat sources (such as stoves or ovens), and lock the front wheel brakes.
2. Check electrical specs to ensure connection to an independent, dedicated **grounded wall outlet** matching the rated voltage (115V/60Hz).
3. Open both doors and adjust the heavy-duty epoxy-coated wire shelves into corresponding side-wall slots based on container heights (e.g., standard GN pans or large food storage boxes).

### 3. Standard Operating Guide

1. **No-Load Test Cooldown**: Plug in and turn on the power switch, letting the empty unit run continuously for **1 to 2 hours**. Only load ingredients after observing the panel temperature drop stably into the 33°F - 39°F range.
2. **Scientific Loading & Partition Management**: Categorize and place meats, vegetables, and prep items on different shelves. **Core Principle: Always leave gaps between food items, and between food, inner walls, and rear air vents.** Never block internal air ducts, as this impedes cold convection and causes localized temperature spikes.
3. **Hot Food Prohibition**: Boiling soups or freshly cooked hot dishes must never go straight into the refrigerator. They must cool naturally to room temperature before sealing and entering the cabinet, preventing sudden internal temperature spikes and excessive condensation.

## IV. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

* **Q: Why do the door frames or side panels of a newly purchased double-door refrigerator feel warm to the touch?**
  * **A:** This is entirely normal commercial refrigerator engineering. To prevent heavy condensation and water droplets from forming along door frames and seams in humid kitchen environments, internal plumbing circuits incorporate a door-frame anti-dew heating function. Once the unit enters steady operation, this warmth will subside.
* **Q: Does a 42.2 cubic-foot large-capacity refrigerator require periodic manual defrosting?**
  * **A:** Not at all. This model features an advanced **dynamic fan-cooling frost-free system** with a built-in automated cyclic defrost program. It automatically evacuates and evaporates trace moisture generated by the evaporator, eliminating traditional refrigerator heavy manual ice scraping and ensuring long-term efficient kitchen operation.
* **Q: Why does opening one door sometimes make the other door feel tightly suctioned shut and hard to pull open?**
  * **A:** This is due to the large internal volume and exceptional sealing of the dual-door refrigerator. When you swiftly close one door, internal air compression or fan-driven action momentarily creates a slight negative pressure differential, causing the opposite door to briefly "seal tight" for a few seconds. This is a sign of superior sealing performance; simply wait 2-3 seconds to easily open it again.
