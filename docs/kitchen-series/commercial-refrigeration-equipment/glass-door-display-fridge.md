### 2. Core Functions & Working Principle

The machine operates efficiently through a combination of a refrigeration system, air delivery system, and merchandising display system:

* **Efficient Circulation Cooling**: The compressor generates cooling capacity, and via the evaporator and built-in fans (fan-cooling models), cold air is evenly blown out from the back or top, forming a cascading cold air current inside the cabinet to ensure uniform cooling speeds between upper and lower shelves with no cooling dead zones.
* **Precision Temperature Control System**: Equipped with a dial-type or microcomputer digital thermostat to automatically monitor internal cabinet temperatures (typically set between 2°C~10°C / 35°F~50°F). When the set temperature is reached, the compressor automatically pauses to reduce store power consumption.

## II. Production Workflow & Standard Operating Procedures (SOP)

To ensure optimal display cooling performance and prevent glass fogging, clerks and operations staff must strictly follow these operational guidelines:

### 1. Site Preparation & Pre-Treatment

* **Heat Dissipation Space Requirements**:
  * The display cooler's compressor unit is typically located at the bottom. You must ensure at least **10 cm (4 inches)** of clearance is reserved between the back of the unit and the wall to prevent the condenser from overheating and causing compressor overload shutdowns.
* **Initial Resting Period Before Powering On**:
  * After receiving and positioning the machine, **DO NOT power it on immediately!** The unit must stand upright and rest for **at least 24 hours** to allow the refrigerant and compressor oil—agitated during transit—to flow back to the bottom before starting up, otherwise permanent refrigeration system damage may occur.

### 2. Pre-Start Inspection & Product Merchandising Guidelines

1. Push the equipment to a flat floor and adjust the leveling feet to keep it completely level, preventing door frame deformation or resonance noise during operation.
2. Ensure the power cord is plugged into a dedicated grounded outlet (for three-door large-capacity units, verify that store electrical capacity matches requirements).
3. **No-Load Cooldown**: Upon initial startup, run the refrigeration system empty for 1.5 - 2 hours, and only start merchandising goods once the cabinet feels distinctly cold inside.

### 3. Standard Stocking & Merchandising Guide (FIFO)

1. **Leave Air-Duct Clearances**: Whether placing beverage bottles or produce, **items at the very back must never touch the rear panel directly**. At least a two-finger width gap must be maintained to ensure smooth downward circulation of cold air.
2. **First-In, First-Out (FIFO) Replenishment**: When restocking room-temperature beverages, move drinks already chilled inside the cabinet to the front row, placing newly replenished room-temperature items in the back. This ensures customers always grab cold drinks while maximizing cooling efficiency.
3. **Minimize Door-Opening Frequency**: Although the equipment has powerful cooling capacity, store staff should still be guided to "restock quickly and in batches" during business hours to avoid leaving glass doors wide open for extended periods.

## IV. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

* **Q: Why does a layer of water mist form on the outside of the glass doors during rainy or humid "south-wind" (Hui Nan) seasons?**
  * **A:** This is a thermal air condensation phenomenon caused by extremely high environmental humidity combined with a large temperature difference between the inside and outside of the cabinet. It is a normal physical state. High-spec display coolers usually feature anti-condensation heating wires around the door frame; you can turn on the corresponding defogging switch. If fogging is heavy, simply wipe the surface gently with a dry cloth without affecting internal food safety.
* **Q: I just placed two cases of room-temperature bottled water inside, why are they still not cold enough after half an hour?**
  * **A:** Water has a high specific heat capacity. Introducing a large batch of room-temperature (or even warm) liquids all at once will instantly absorb the cold capacity inside the cabinet. Initial cooling for large batches of beverages typically requires a 2 to 4-hour running period. Merchants are advised to replenish room-temperature stock in bulk before closing shop at night so that everything is fully chilled by the following morning.
* **Q: Is it normal to hear a "humming" and trickling sound while the cabinet is running?**
  * **A:** Yes, it is normal. The humming sound comes from the operation of the heavy-duty commercial compressor and condensing fan; the trickling sound is the refrigerant circulating through the pipelines, transitioning from gas to liquid state—both are normal physical processes of mechanical refrigeration.
