## 6. Workflow

### Production Flow (6-Step Standard Operation)

```
Step 1 Step 2 Step 3 Step 4 Step 5 Step 6
Hopper Loading → Bottle Infeed → Photocell Count → 4-Nozzle Piston → Anti-Drip Cut-off → Bottle Discharge
& Preparation (Conveyor) & Stop Positioning Dosing (Positive Shutoff) (Capping / Labeling)
(80 L hopper) (2 m belt) (SICK sensor) (Pneumatic+Electric) (Finished)
```

#### Step 1 — Hopper Loading & Preparation

- **Equipment**: 80 L hopper + PLC touchscreen
- **Operation**: Fill the 80 L hopper with the material to be packed, connect the air compressor (0.6 – 0.8 MPa), switch on the power and set the filling volume and filling speed on the touchscreen.
- **Key Point**: Ensure the material is free of lumps and hard particles for pastes; set the filling volume before starting so the first cycle is already accurate.

#### Step 2 — Bottle Infeed

- **Equipment**: 2 m conveyor belt with bottle-fixing device
- **Operation**: Empty bottles are loaded onto the conveyor and automatically carried into the filling station; the belt's fixing device keeps every bottle stable during filling.
- **Key Point**: Keep bottle spacing even and orientation consistent so the photocell can count reliably.

#### Step 3 — Photocell Counting & Stop Positioning

- **Equipment**: German SICK photocell sensor + bottle-stop cylinder
- **Operation**: The SICK sensor counts bottles as they arrive. Once 4 bottles are in position, the bottle-stop cylinder extends to lock them precisely under the nozzles.
- **Key Point**: If a bottle is missing, the no-bottle-no-fill logic prevents the cycle from running — no product is wasted.

#### Step 4 — 4-Nozzle Piston Dosing

- **Equipment**: 4 filling nozzles, each with an independent cylinder
- **Operation**: The four filling heads descend together and the pistons push the material out, filling all 4 bottles simultaneously with 1% accuracy in one cycle.
- **Key Point**: Filling volume and speed are adjustable through the man-machine interface; because each head has its own cylinder, all four nozzles stay consistent. The same machine handles both liquids and pastes.

#### Step 5 — Anti-Drip Cut-off

- **Equipment**: Bottom-close positive shutoff nozzles
- **Operation**: At the end of each cycle the positive shutoff valve closes instantly and the nozzles lift away cleanly.
- **Key Point**: The bottom-close design eliminates dripping even at 40 cycles/min, protecting bottle mouths and labels from contamination.

#### Step 6 — Bottle Discharge

- **Equipment**: Outlet conveyor + downstream equipment
- **Operation**: The bottle-stop cylinder retracts and the filled bottles move out along the conveyor to the next process.
- **Key Point**: For a full line, match the discharge conveyor speed with the capping machine to avoid jams. Downstream options include cap tightening, capping and labeling machines.

### Production-Line Recommendations

- **Operators**: 1 – 2 (1 monitoring the touchscreen, 1 supplying bottles and performing visual quality check)
- **Cycle Time**: 10 – 40 times/min × 4 nozzles
- **Ceiling Requirement**: Allow space for the 80 L hopper when hopper-filling from above
- **Supporting Equipment**: Air compressor 0.6 – 0.8 MPa, 300 – 400 L/min
- **Floor Space**: Plan for the machine footprint plus a 2 m conveyor run and access space on both sides
- **Changeover**: Product changeover is done entirely on the touchscreen — filling volume and speed only, no mechanical parts to swap
- **Model Selection**: Choose the model by the upper end of the customer's bottle sizes — for bottles from 200 to 800 ml, the FA4-1000 gives the best accuracy coverage; customers filling both liquids and pastes are covered by this series alone
