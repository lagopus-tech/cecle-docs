### IV. Sales Strategy & FAQ

#### **Sales Strategy**
* **The "Cost-Per-Label" Angle**: If a client currently uses adhesive stickers, calculate their monthly spend on stickers vs. sleeve film. Emphasize that the film material is cheaper and the ROI on the machine is rapid.
* **The "Safety & Aesthetics" Selling Point**: For clients in food/beverage, push the tamper-evident aspect (neck band). For cosmetic/irregular bottle clients, highlight that the steam shrinkage creates a professional, "full-bottle" graphic finish that stickers cannot achieve.
* **The "Manual to Auto" Migration**: Highlight the flexibility—if they aren't ready for 150pcs/min, they can buy the steam tunnel for manual operation, giving them a growth path without requiring a massive initial investment.

#### **Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)**
* **Q: Why choose a steam shrink tunnel over a standard hot-air tunnel?**
    * A: Steam shrinking is more consistent and gentler. It prevents the label from melting or distorting and ensures an even shrink on irregular bottle surfaces where hot air might cause uneven results.
* **Q: Can I use this for just the bottle cap?**
    * A: Yes. This machine is widely used for applying tamper-evident bands to bottle necks for product safety.
* **Q: What if my production volume is low right now?**
    * A: You can start by manually sleeve-applying the labels and using the ST-18 Steam Tunnel to shrink them. As your business grows, you can add the SL-150 automatic applicator.

### VI. Workflow
1. **Bottle Infeed**: Bottles are conveyed into the SL-150 unit via the automated conveyor belt.
2. **Label Application**: The machine automatically measures the bottle position, cuts the film to the precise length, and sleeves it over the bottle (either neck or full body).
3. **Steam Shrinking**: The sleeved bottles enter the ST-18 Steam Tunnel, where high-pressure steam circulates to shrink the label firmly and uniformly around the container.
4. **Final Inspection**: The labeled bottles exit the tunnel, cool rapidly (ensuring label stability), and are ready for downstream processes like secondary packing or cartoning.
