# Date Coding Machine Selection Guide and Product Overview

## I. What is a Date Coding Machine
### 1.1 Core Definition
A Date Coding Machine (or Date Printer) is a specialized device used to clearly print critical information—such as the Manufacturing Date (MFG), Expiry Date (EXP), Batch Number (LOT), and weight—on various product packaging (e.g., plastic film bags, paper boxes, aluminum foil bags, adhesive labels, and cards). The entire series abandons traditional liquid ink in favor of thermal ribbons or solid ink rollers, achieving an instant-dry effect. It is an essential tool for the food, pharmaceutical, and daily chemical industries to meet packaging compliance requirements.

### 1.2 Working Principles
Based on the operation and drive mode, the core coding processes are divided into three categories:
1. **Manual / Semi-Automatic Thermal Ribbon Printing (DY-8 / HP-241B)**: The operator manually places the packaging material. Driven by a pure manual lever or an electric motor (foot pedal/continuous mode), the heated brass character mold presses down, instantly melting the high-temperature coating of the thermal ribbon and transferring it to the packaging material.
2. **Pneumatic Inline Synchronized Printing (HP-241C)**: The coder is integrated into vertical or flow wrapping packaging machines. It receives photoelectric or cam signals from the main packaging machine and uses a pneumatic cylinder to press down, completing the coding synchronously during the intermittent movement of the packaging film.
3. **Solid Ink Automatic Roller Printing (MY-380F)**: Utilizing photoelectric control and an automatic paging structure, a stack of unformed, flat packaging (empty boxes/labels) is placed in the feeding hopper. The machine automatically separates and feeds them at high speed, while a heated, melting solid ink roller rolls over the character wheel to print characters onto the packaging.

### 1.3 Core Values
- **Ink-Free & Zero Pollution**: Uses thermal ribbons or solid ink rollers. It dries instantly, keeps hands clean, and perfectly meets the strict hygiene standards of the food and pharmaceutical industries.
- **Strong Adhesion**: Thermal transfer technology allows the print to firmly adhere to flexible packaging surfaces, making it highly resistant to scratching or erasing.
- **Flexible Typography**: Comes with a rich set of brass character molds, supporting single, double, or even triple-row layouts with freely combinable letters and numbers.
- **Wide Scenario Coverage**: Covers a full range of applications, from portable manual coding for retail shops/workshops to desktop semi-automatic coding for factories, and high-speed offline/inline batch production.

### 1.4 Applicable Industries
- **Food Industry**: Snack bags, bread bags, tea foil bags, candy wrappers.
- **Pharmaceutical Industry**: Medicine boxes (empty), medical consumable plastic bags, label paper.
- **Daily Chemicals & Agriculture**: Cosmetic tube labels, toiletry boxes, seed bags, fertilizer bags.
- **Specific Scenarios**: Manual coding for startups, semi-automatic batch production for small-to-medium workshops, automated packaging line integration, and high-speed offline pre-coding for bulk empty boxes/labels.

## III. Core Selection Advice

### 3.1 By Capacity & Automation Requirements
1. **Hundreds to a couple thousand prints/day**: Choose the **DY-8** (Manual). Highest cost-performance, easiest to operate.
2. **Thousands of prints/day, need to reduce fatigue**: Choose the **HP-241B** (Electric Semi-Auto). Easily switch between foot pedal and continuous mode.
3. **Tens of thousands of empty boxes/bags/labels**: Choose the **MY-380F** (Automatic Solid Ink Coder) for extremely fast offline coding at 300 pcs/min.
4. **Already own an automatic packaging machine and need inline printing**: You must select the **HP-241C** (Pneumatic Synchronized).

### 3.2 By Packaging Material Status
- **Soft bags, boxes, or sheets already filled with product**: Use DY-8 or HP-241B (place on the silicone pad of the base to code).
- **Completely flat empty paper boxes, single sheet labels, unsealed film bags**: Highly recommended for the MY-380F for high-speed auto-paging. **(Note: If the packaging is already filled/bulging or has an uneven surface, the MY-380F CANNOT be used.)**
- **Continuous roll film (before cutting/forming)**: Suitable for HP-241C integrated onto a packaging machine.

### 3.3 By Facility Requirements
- **Standard electricity only (110V/220V), no air compressor**: DY-8, HP-241B, and MY-380F are plug-and-play.
- **Has an air compressor and needs to sync with machinery**: HP-241C Pneumatic Coder.

## IV. General Technical FAQ

### 4.1 Consumables & Print Quality
**Q: Do these machines need liquid ink? Will the print smear?**
A: None of the series require liquid ink. The DY-8 and HP-241 series use thermal ribbons; the MY-380F uses solid ink rollers. Both use heat to melt and transfer the pigment, resulting in instant drying. You can rub it immediately after printing without smearing. Extremely clean and hygienic.

**Q: What should I do if the print is too light or unclear?**
A: This usually happens for two reasons: 1. Insufficient temperature. Please turn up the temperature control knob (especially for the MY-380F, which MUST be preheated for 10-15 minutes to fully melt the ink roller). 2. The ribbon or ink roller is running out and needs to be replaced.

### 4.2 Character Molds & Typography
**Q: Do the machines come with character molds? Can I print "EXP Date"?**
A: Both the DY-8 and HP-241B come standard with a box of universal brass characters (including multiple sets of 0-9, and letters like EXP/LOT/MFG), allowing you to easily spell out formats like "EXP 2025/12/31".

**Q: Are the character molds interchangeable between different models?**
A: The standard brass characters for the DY-8 and HP-241 series (usually 2×3×15mm or 2×4×15mm) are interchangeable. However, the **MY-380F** uses a high-speed roller structure and requires specific T-type or R-type brass characters, which are NOT interchangeable with the others.

### 4.3 Replacement & Maintenance
**Q: How long does a ribbon last? How can I save ribbon?**
A: A roll of ribbon can typically print thousands of times. The HP-241B/HP-241C models feature a "butterfly screw for spacing adjustment" on the side. You can finely tune the stepping distance after each print based on your font size to minimize ribbon waste.

**Q: What are the daily wear parts for the MY-380F?**
A: Mainly the solid hot ink roller and the rubber transmission belts. It is highly recommended to purchase a few extra sets when buying the machine. Replacement is very simple.

## VI. Standard Operating Procedure (Example: Semi-Automatic HP-241B)

1. **Setting the Type**: Pull out the character mold head, use tweezers to arrange the required brass characters (e.g., EXP 25 10 12) into the slot, tighten the positioning screw, push the head back into the machine, and lock it.
2. **Threading the Ribbon**: Follow the ribbon routing diagram on the side of the machine. Thread the entire roll of ribbon through the guide shafts and tension wheels, and attach the end to the waste ribbon collection spool.
3. **Power On & Preheat**: Plug into a 220V/110V power source, turn on the heating switch, adjust the temperature knob to level 3-5 (depending on bag thickness), and **wait 5-10 minutes for it to heat up**.
4. **Parameter Debugging**:
   - Adjust the "ribbon stepping screw" to ensure that the ribbon advances just enough to cover the used area after each press, preventing waste.
   - Test print on a few empty bags placed on the base pad. If the print is too light, increase the temperature or pressure; if the packaging melts/burns, decrease the temperature.
5. **Official Production**: Place the material flat on the coding area. Depending on your proficiency, either use the "foot pedal" for single prints or switch to the "Auto" gear and feed materials continuously to the machine's rhythm.
6. **Shutdown & Maintenance**: After production, turn off the heating switch and power. Wait for the character head to cool down completely before cleaning up any dust or debris around the machine.
