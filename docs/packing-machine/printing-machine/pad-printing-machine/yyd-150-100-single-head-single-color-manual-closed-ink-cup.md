## IV. Operation & Workflow

### 1. Required Materials
* **Substrate**: Small/medium flat or slightly curved workpieces made of metal, glass, ceramic, wood, leather, and similar materials.
* **Consumables**: Steel sheet, fixture, rubber head, ink, thinner, ink cup doctor ring.

### 2. Standard Workflow
1. **Steel Sheet & Rubber Head Installation**: Lay the etched steel sheet flat and fix it on the steel-sheet base, then mount the rubber head of the corresponding size.
2. **Ink Filling**: Pour a proper amount of prepared ink into the sealed ink cup, install the cup, and confirm the ceramic ring fits well against the steel sheet.
3. **Workpiece Placement**: Place the workpiece on the worktable and complete alignment.
4. **Trial Printing & Adjustment**: Press the handle to complete a test print, observe pattern sharpness, and fine-tune as needed.
5. **Mass Production**: After adjustment, continuously press the handle manually for printing; it is recommended to check the ink level in the cup and the rubber head cleanliness every few prints.
6. **Shutdown & Maintenance**: After work, remove residual ink from the cup, wipe the rubber head, steel sheet, and cup with cleaning agent, and keep the machine dry and clean.

### FAQ
* **Q: Does this machine need to be connected to power or an air compressor?**
 * **A**: No. The YYD-150-100 is purely manual operation, requiring no power supply or compressed air — simply place it on a desk and use it.
* **Q: What is the difference between ink cup and inkwell (oil pan) types?**
 * **A**: The ink cup type uses a sealed ink cup plus a ceramic ring, making it more eco-friendly with less solvent evaporation and lower ink consumption; the inkwell type is an open structure that usually costs less but has more solvent evaporation. Small-batch customers and those with higher environmental requirements are better suited to the ink cup type.
