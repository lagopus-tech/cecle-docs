### 2. Core Functions & Working Principles

* **Thermal Sealing**: Once air extraction is complete, the suction nozzle retracts automatically while the heating element rapidly heats up, tightly fusing and pressing the bag opening to form a high-integrity airtight seal.
* **Cooling & Molding**: The sealing area undergoes a brief cooling and solidification cycle to ensure a flat, leak-free seal, automatically concluding the entire working cycle.

## 2. Standard Operating Procedures (SOP) & Production Workflow

To ensure optimal vacuum performance and sealing quality while extending the service life of the machine, please strictly follow these operating protocols:

### 1. Bag Selection & Material Preparation

* **Choose the Right Packaging Bags**: External vacuum machines typically require **Embossed Vacuum Bags** or specialized bags with micro-channel venting structures to ensure smooth air exhaust.
* **Leave Headspace**: Do not overfill the packaging bag; it is recommended to leave at least 4–6 cm of empty space at the top to ensure the bag opening can fit smoothly over the suction nozzle.
* **Keep the Bag Opening Clean**: The sealing area of the bag must remain dry, clean, and free of oil stains or debris to prevent weak seals or air leaks caused by trapped contaminants.

### 2. Control Panel Parameter Settings

1. **Power On**: Turn on the main power switch of the machine.
2. **Set Vacuum Time**: Adjusted according to bag size and air volume, generally set between **5 to 15 seconds**.
3. **Set Sealing Time**: Adjusted based on the material and thickness of the packaging bag, usually between **1.5 to 3.0 seconds**.
4. **Set Cooling Time**: Generally set to **2.0 to 3.0 seconds** to allow the sealing strip to solidify properly under pressure, ensuring a neat and firm seal.

### 3. Routine Cleaning & Maintenance

* **Keep the Suction Nozzle Clean**: After each use, check the suction nozzle for liquid intake or debris blockages, and periodically wipe it down with a clean, damp cloth to keep air pathways unobstructed.
* **Inspect Heating Strips and Teflon Cloth**: If scorching, tearing, or bag-sticking occurs at the sealing seam, promptly replace the Teflon high-resistance adhesive tape and heating wire.

## 4. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

* **Q: What is the difference between an external vacuum machine and a chamber (internal) vacuum machine? Which one is better?**
  * **A**: The biggest advantage of an external vacuum machine is that **there are no size limits imposed by a vacuum chamber**—as long as the bag fits the sealing bar length, items of any size can be vacuum-sealed. In contrast, chamber machines require the entire item to fit inside an enclosed chamber. If you need to package bulky, irregular, or long items, the external type is the best choice.
* **Q: Why do external vacuum machines typically require "embossed bags"?**
  * **A**: Because external machines use a suction nozzle that extends directly into the bag, using completely smooth ordinary plastic bags would cause the two walls of the bag to collapse and tightly stick together under suction, blocking the nozzle mouth and trapping air inside. Embossed bags feature micro-textures on the surface that maintain venting channels, allowing air to be completely evacuated.
* **Q: Can this machine be used to package liquids containing soups/juices?**
  * **A**: External vacuum machines are primarily designed for solids, dry goods, and meat products. Packaging items with high liquid content can easily cause liquids to be sucked into the air pump, damaging the machine. If you need to package large quantities of liquids or soups, we recommend choosing our "Chamber Vacuum Packing Machine (DZ Series)".
