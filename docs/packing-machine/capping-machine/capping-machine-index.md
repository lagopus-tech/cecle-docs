# Selection Guide & Product Overview: Capping Machines (Full Series)

## 1. What is a Capping Machine?

**Working Principle**
A capping machine uses a power source (Electric or Pneumatic) to drive a capping head that generates mechanical torque to tighten caps onto containers.
*   **Friction Capping:** Uses a high-speed rotating rubber head to tighten caps via friction (e.g., SGJ-80, SG-1550).
*   **Locking/Rolling:** Specifically designed for anti-theft or aluminum caps, using physical side-pressure to lock the ring (e.g., DK-50).
*   **Vacuum Sealing:** Extracts air from a sealed chamber before capping to extend food shelf life (e.g., VC-10A).
*   **Crimping/Clamping:** Used for perfume pumps or vials to achieve a permanent, tamper-evident seal (e.g., PC-88).

**Core Value**
*   **Sealing Consistency:** Ensures every bottle has uniform torque, eliminating leakage or loose caps caused by manual labor.
*   **Efficiency Scalability:** Covers everything from "low-volume lab sampling" to "large-scale factory automation."
*   **High Compatibility:** Specially designed heads adapt to pump heads, sprayers, duckbill caps, and other irregular shapes.

**Target Industries**
Widely used in Cosmetics, Daily Chemicals, Pharmaceuticals, Food & Beverage, Lubricant Oil, and Chemical Raw Materials.

## 3. Technical FAQ & Common Solutions

**Q: Both SGJ-80 and DK-50 are desktop models. How do I choose?**
*   **A:** It depends entirely on the **cap structure**. **SGJ-80** uses friction and is perfect for pump heads or sprayers with long straws. **DK-50** uses a mechanical roller to lock the cap ring, designed specifically for rigid plastic caps with "anti-theft break-away rings."

**Q: What vacuum level can the VC-10A achieve?**
*   **A:** When paired with a professional vacuum pump, it can exceed **0.08Mpa**. This effectively inhibits aerobic bacteria growth, making it essential for glass jars with tinplate caps (e.g., pickles, bird's nest, jams).

**Q: Why choose a handheld SG-1550 over a desktop machine?**
*   **A:** It is the ultimate solution for **spatial constraints**. When containers are too large to move (e.g., 20L drums) or too heavy/irregular for a worktable, the handheld unit—supported by a spring balancer—offers unmatched flexibility.

**Q: What is the difference between ASGJ and ADK on a production line?**
*   **A:** **ASGJ** focuses on "flexibility"; it allows for fast mold changes and is suited for manufacturers with many SKUs and medium batches. **ADK** focuses on "maximum output"; it features synchronized multi-head capping for mature, high-volume dedicated lines.
